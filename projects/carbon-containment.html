Carbon Containment Lab

Predictive model for methane emission detection from orphaned oil wells

January 2026 - Present • UC Berkeley

Overview

Building a predictive model for methane emission detection from orphaned oil wells. Orphaned wells — oil and gas wells that have been abandoned without proper plugging — are a significant source of methane emissions, a potent greenhouse gas. Identifying which wells pose the greatest risk is critical for prioritizing remediation efforts.

Geospatial Analysis

Applying geospatial analysis and statistical modeling to prioritize well remediation. By analyzing spatial patterns in well locations, geological formations, and historical production data, the model identifies wells most likely to be emitting methane at significant rates.

Royalty Model

Creating a royalty model to incentivize plugging abandoned wells. This economic model explores how financial incentives can be structured to encourage private operators and landowners to participate in well remediation programs, potentially creating a self-sustaining market for well plugging.

Methodology

The project combines:

  • Statistical modeling to predict methane emission rates from well characteristics
  • Geospatial analysis using satellite and ground-based measurement data
  • Economic modeling to design incentive structures
  • Data pipeline development for processing well inventory databases
