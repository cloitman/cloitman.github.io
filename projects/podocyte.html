Podocyte Visualization

Published research visualizing the 3D structure of kidney cells

May 2017 - August 2018; Washington University in St. Louis

Overview

I worked in a nephrology lab studying how kidney disease changes the filtration barrier in our kidneys: the glomerular basement membrane and the podocytes that sit on it. Using Python and Amira 3D visualization software, I built 3D models of podocytes and their actin networks from electron microscopy images, and I also helped with DNA sequencing as part of the lab's other work.

Publication

Qu C, Roth R, Puapatanakul P, Loitman C, Hammad D, Genin GM, Miner JH, Suleiman HY. "Three-Dimensional Visualization of the Podocyte Actin Network Using Integrated Membrane Extraction, Electron Microscopy, and Machine Learning." J Am Soc Nephrol. 2022 Jan;33(1):155-173.

Background

Podocytes are kidney cells whose foot processes, held in shape by an internal actin network, wrap around the glomerular basement membrane and help filter blood. Many kidney diseases disrupt this structure, leading to proteinuria and other complications, but its 3D organization is hard to see. The lab used focused ion beam–scanning electron microscopy (FIB-SEM): the microscope images a thin layer of the sample, a focused ion beam mills that layer away, and the process repeats until thousands of images can be stacked into a 3D model.

My Contributions

I analyzed thousands of these images, manually identifying structures before using Python and Amira to segment and render them in 3D. Over a little more than a year, I built the 3D models of FIB-SEM images behind Figures 5–8 of the published paper. Separately from the paper, I helped conduct DNA sequencing as part of the lab's other work.
