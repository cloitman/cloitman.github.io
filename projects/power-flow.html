Power Flow Modelling

Analyzing electric transmission systems using optimal power flow and capacity expansion models

August 2025 - May 2026 • UC Berkeley

Overview

Analyzing electric transmission systems using optimal power flow (OPF) and capacity expansion models as part of UC Berkeley's Electric Transmission Systems coursework. This work studies grid reliability and the challenges of integrating renewable energy sources into existing transmission infrastructure.

Optimal Power Flow

OPF models determine the most economical way to dispatch generation while satisfying physical constraints of the power network, including line flow limits, voltage constraints, and generator capacity limits. The formulations are implemented in Julia using the JuMP optimization framework.

Capacity Expansion

Capacity expansion models determine optimal investment decisions for new generation and transmission infrastructure over multi-year planning horizons. These models balance capital costs against operational savings and reliability requirements, incorporating uncertainty in demand growth and renewable generation profiles.

Renewable Integration

A key focus is understanding how high penetrations of variable renewable energy (wind and solar) impact grid operations and what transmission investments are needed to accommodate the energy transition. This includes studying congestion patterns, curtailment, and the value of storage and flexible demand.
