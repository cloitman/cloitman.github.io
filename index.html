About Me

I'm an engineer focused on powering the green energy transition. I want to unlock capacity through batteries, heat pumps, and thermostats by operating them like any other part of our power system.

I currently operate batteries and solar farms at Tyba Energy by forecasting prices and optimizing decision making according to market signals. My work spans electric transmission system analysis, data science, and game theory.

I received the Master of Climate Solutions at UC Berkeley, where I did many projects related to battery siting in class and independent study. I am writing a first-author working paper from my capacity expansion planning research with the Energy Modelling, Analysis, and Control Group at UC Berkeley.

I spent a few years trading commodities like carbon credits and energy and focusing on improving execution of strategies.

I hold a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering and a B.A. in Economics from Yale University, where I received the Lichty & Waters Prize and the Outstanding Undergraduate Achievement Award.

Virtual Power Plant Research

Battery allocation, arbitrage optimization, and residential storage deployment strategy — CEE295, ENERES 254, and SunStrong Management

Two academic papers develop convex optimization (CVXPY) frameworks for battery siting and arbitrage profit maximization on a 13-node IEEE radial feeder, using real CAISO day-ahead market LMP data. A third project applies this work — alongside financial modeling and California policy research — to produce deployment recommendations for SunStrong Management, which leases approximately 500,000 residential solar systems. Key findings: battery siting converges to the most congested network node; optimal capacity saturates around 22–28 MWh; and SDG&E territory offers the best risk-adjusted returns for residential battery rollout.

About Me

I'm a mechanical engineer and quantitative researcher focused on energy systems and the clean energy transition. I combine engineering fundamentals with data science and machine learning to tackle challenges in power systems, renewable energy, and grid optimization.

I recently finished a Master of Climate Solutions at UC Berkeley. I previously studied mechanical engineering at Yale, where I conducted materials science research and gained experience in quantitative finance.

Now, I am uniting my interests in data science, engineering, and sustainability as a Quantitative Researcher at Tyba Energy, where I research and operate battery and solar portfolios. I am also writing up my power systems research from the Energy Modelling, Analysis, and Control Group at UC Berkeley as a first-author working paper. I hope to contribute to novel VPPs, improve distributed energy resources, and ensure the stability of our power grid.

Background

I grew up in St. Louis, MO and am a proud fan of the Cardinals and the Blues. I love the Midwest and will happily tell everyone about the wonderful museums and great food back home. I spent most of the summers when I was younger canoeing in the Boundary Waters Canoe Area in Northern Minnesota which gave me a passion for nature and conservation.

Starting in middle school, I was an active member of Science Olympiad and decided early on in high school I would become an engineer to contribute to renewables to combine my interests in sustainability and science. At Yale, I chose mechanical engineering to learn about the engineering opportunities and constraints of energy systems and contributed to materials science research to support solar panels, which was where I first became exposed to data science.

Hoping to learn about the incentives that allow the proliferation of fossil fuels, I decided to take a game theory class. I loved the mathematical modelling aspects of the subject and decided to add an economics degree where I also studied resource economics. During COVID, an economics professor invited me to work at his hedge fund where I applied my data science background to optimization algorithms for options pricing.

Still passionate about energy but with an interest in quantitative research methods I learned at the hedge fund, I decided to trade commodities at Flow Traders. I had success building out energy trading frameworks and I even built out the firm’s first carbon credit trading strategies. Nonetheless, I wanted more environmental impact with my work and closer application of engineering principles, so I returned to school at UC Berkeley. Through my coursework, I have developed an expertise in power systems modelling and markets which I will apply to improve the efficiency and profitability of renewables.

What I'm Working On

At Tyba Energy, I build battery and solar co-optimization strategies in CAISO and real-time and ancillary-service bidding models in ERCOT and MISO. I also developed a risk-sharing model that lets battery and renewable operators trade with each other to reduce variance in returns. Beyond Tyba, I’m interested in the application of storage and controls to develop virtual power plants, and I am preparing a first-author working paper on downscaling regional load projections for capacity expansion planning.
